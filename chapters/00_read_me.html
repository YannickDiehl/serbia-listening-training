What this report documents, how to read it, and what happens in each chapter. Computes nothing.

The study

What was studied? Social workers in 20 Serbian municipalities took part in a listening training. Ten municipalities were trained between January and March 2025; the other ten waited and were trained between September and December 2025. Participants answered the same questionnaire up to four times: in January (T0), March (T1), September (T2) and December (T3). The analyses test whether the training improved listening and togetherness (H1), cognitive complexity (H2) and well-being (H3).

How to read this report

How do code and results relate?

  • Every chapter shows the code that produces its results, directly above them. Code that several chapters share is kept in a separate file and shown in full where it is used.
  • The text explains each step in plain language. Knowing R helps, but the logic can be followed without it.
  • Each chapter names the tables and sections of the paper its results feed.
  • Numbers that the paper reports are checked in the code: a hidden line stops the report if one of them changes.

Can the analyses be re-run? The questionnaires carry self-generated participant codes, ages and sex, so the data are not published with this report. The code is complete: every step can be followed and applied to comparable data.

Who is SW0123? Every person appears under a pseudonym: the respondent ID of the data deposit (SW0001 to SW0461, and SW0462 for the second of two people who shared one code), or T01 to T64 for a code that was typed at one wave and corrected later. The codes themselves, built from the mother’s name and birthday, never appear in this report.

What each chapter contains

Part A: Data

  1. From questionnaires to the analysis file. Reads the 79 Excel files returned by the municipalities, builds the participant codes, corrects codes that were mistyped, removes paper forms that were entered twice, and saves one analysis file.
  2. Scales and analysis datasets. Reverse-codes negatively worded items, builds the 23 scale scores and the three hypothesis composites, and arranges the data one row per questionnaire and one row per person.
  3. Sample. Who took part, at which waves and in which municipalities; whether the two conditions are comparable; the sample sets used to test robustness.
  4. Data quality. Screens every questionnaire for careless responding and searches once more for forms entered twice.

Part B: Measurement

  1. Reliability and baseline correlations. Internal consistency of every scale and composite at each wave, and how the scales relate before the training.
  2. Measurement models per wave. Confirmatory factor analysis of every scale at each of the four waves.
  3. Measurement invariance over time. Whether each scale measures the same construct in the same way at all four waves, the precondition for comparing means over time; judged by two sets of cutoffs, with partial invariance, approximate invariance (alignment) and a test across the two groups before the training.
  4. Simultaneous block models. All scales of one hypothesis modelled together, to test whether they are distinct.

Part C: Training effects, as preregistered

  1. Means and Δd. Means by condition and wave, and the standardized difference in change between the trained and the waiting group, with a second standard deviation, a correction for multiple testing and a look at dropout.
  2. Multilevel models. The preregistered models of the training effect, with participants nested in municipalities and, as a check, in trainers; one-sided tests and a correction for multiple testing; the replication in the second round; random slopes; equivalence tests.
  3. Latent difference-in-differences. The training effect on latent means, corrected for measurement error; with the control group’s change while it waits, a correction for multiple testing, a restricted model and the effects across the sample sets.
  4. Serial mediation. Whether the training improves well-being through listening and cognitive complexity, with manifest composites and with latent blocks.
  5. Movers. How many participants changed reliably, by condition.
  6. Robustness. Every effect re-estimated in alternative samples: complete cases, attrition patterns, careless responding.

Part D: Post hoc

  1. Item pruning. Which items were set aside, for ceiling effects or low responsiveness, and why.
  2. Exploratory graph analysis. The dimensional structure of the item pool, estimated from the data.
  3. Redundancy and IRT. Redundant items and item response models, also at each wave.
  4. Final structure. The resulting item structure and its replication across the four waves.
  5. Training effects on the new structure. The training effects re-estimated on the factors found in the data and on the single item on life satisfaction, and whether the factors are measured invariantly over time.
  6. Synthesis. How the preregistered and the post hoc results fit together.

Part E: Appendix

  1. Study 1. The train-the-trainer study with the 16 trainers; it follows once its data are available.
  2. Item-level Δd. The training effect on every single item.
  3. Software versions.
  4. Requests and their status. What the team asked for in its e-mails, in plain words, and where each request stands.
