24. Requests and their status

NoteAbout this chapter

What the team asked for in its e-mails about the analyses and about this documentation, from June 2025 to November 2026, and where each request stands now. The list was compiled from the e-mails and checked against the chapters of this documentation. Organisational matters and the wording of the paper are not part of it; requests about the paper are only summarised at the end. Computes nothing.

24.1 How the list was made

Where do the requests come from? The team discussed the analyses in 52 e-mail threads between June 2025 and November 2026. Every request in them was noted, 269 in all: questions, proposals, corrections and the tasks of the work plans.

Which requests are left out? Of these, 56 were organisational, about travel, funding, meetings and submissions; they are left out. Another 49 concern the paper rather than this documentation, such as the text of its sections, its figures or the literature; they are summarised in 24.14. Each of the other 164 requests was checked against the chapters: is the analysis there, in which section, and in the form that was asked for?

How is a request written here? In one plain sentence, in the words of this documentation rather than those of the e-mail. Requests that ask for the same thing share a row. The column Where names the section, for example 9.3 for Chapter 9, Section 3; a dash marks a request that concerns the whole documentation or that no section answers.

What do the statuses mean?

  • done: the documentation does what was asked, in the section named.
  • partly done: part of it is there; the table says what is missing.
  • open: not done yet.
  • decided otherwise: another solution was chosen; the table gives the reason.
  • no longer needed: overtaken by later work, for example by a model that replaced the one the request was about.
  • concerns the paper: belongs to the manuscript, not to this documentation (24.14).

A row may merge several requests, and one request may lead to two rows, so the rows of the tables below do not add up to these counts. Where a request leaves a choice, the documentation shows the alternatives side by side; 24.13 lists the choices that are still to be made.

24.13 Decisions that remain open

Which choices are still to be made? For six questions the documentation shows the alternatives side by side, so that the team can decide with the results in view:

  • Cutoffs for invariance. Whether the conventional cutoffs or the stricter ones for small samples decide; the verdicts differ for a single construct (Chapter 7.3).
  • Multiple testing and one-sided tests. Whether the results are corrected for testing many scales at once, and whether they are tested one-sided, as the directional hypotheses would allow (Chapters 9.5, 10.7 and 11.4).
  • The standard deviation of Δd. Whether Δd is standardized by the persons who answered at both waves of a window or by all questionnaires before it; the choice matters mainly for the replication while the control group trains (Chapter 9.4).
  • Trainers in the multilevel models. Whether the models include the trainers as a level, as preregistered, although the trainers add almost nothing and change no verdict (Chapters 10.2 and 10.6).
  • Latent models at a boundary and missing covariates. How to treat the seven latent models with an estimate at an impossible value, such as a negative variance, and the 23 persons without age or sex, who are not part of the latent comparisons (Chapter 11.2).
  • Post hoc factors. How to report the effects on the two post hoc factors, which are not measured alike at all four waves, so that their effects rest on manifest scores only (Chapter 19.6).
